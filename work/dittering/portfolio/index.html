01 / The brief

How simple can a picture get?

A picture is a grid of squares. Make the squares bigger and it gets simpler. Swap a thousand colours for ten and it gets simpler again. At some point it stops being the picture, and just before that it is at its most interesting.

The brief to myself: build a tool that finds that point, and a brand that looks like it.

  • LookShrink to a size where every cell is countable.
  • CountGroup the colours into about ten.
  • NameTomato. Marigold. Deep chartreuse.
  • SquashSnap every cell to its nearest colour.
  • PrintSet it as a poster, with the numbers on it.

02 / Brand

A ring that never quite closes.

The mark is a ring on a 20 by 20 grid with an ordered-dithered edge. It is the same ring the landing page scatters under your cursor. The wordmark is Bricolage Grotesque 800, tightened, in sentence case.

Colour is flat and loud: ink, paper, one tomato, and four data colours. When a gradient is needed it is resolved into cells, the ladder.

03 / Posters

Twelve layouts. Seventeen colour looks.

The app picks a layout from the picture itself: panoramas melt, bold shapes get a silhouette, busy ones get a contact sheet. Every colour in the print is taken from the picture. Click the poster and you get another.

04 / In the world

Colour, in motion.

Three poster studies dissolve inside a gallery frame. Below, a freestanding display and a floating triptych give the artwork space, depth and movement.

05 / Zine

Squint, the zine.

Field Notes: a refreshed sixteen-page edition with an oversized typographic cover, an introduction to the process, thirteen artwork studies and a closing colophon. Generous margins and consistent folios give each image room to breathe.

06 / Product and stationery

Phone, laptop, paper.

The app is one screen and a dock: previous and next layout, colour scheme, rotate, hold to squint, caption, download. Everything else on the brand follows the same rule, fewer things at a bigger size.
